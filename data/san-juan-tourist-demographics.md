# San Juan Tourist Demographics — Research Brief

**Site:** san-juan-excursions.com (EN + ES)
**Sources:** Puerto Rico Institute of Statistics + Puerto Rico Tourism Company Visitor Profile FY2023-24 (primary), Discover Puerto Rico industry research FY2025, San Juan Cruise Port statistics, Skyscanner/Expedia search data, Discover Puerto Rico IPW 2026 European market report.

---

## 1. Origin split: US mainland vs international

**Headline:** **93% US / 7% international** (FY2023-24, official survey data, n=23,782).

| Source | % of all PR visitors |
|---|---|
| United States (total) | 93% |
| International (non-US) | 7% |
| Of which: Puerto Ricans residing in other US jurisdictions | 63% of total |

**International visitor top countries** (only ~842 obs — small sample):
Dominican Republic, Colombia, US Virgin Islands, Canada, Mexico, **Spain**, Chile, Costa Rica, Panama, UK.

**US state breakdown** (top feeders): FL 33%, NY 10%, TX 7%, NJ/PA/MA/GA/IL/CT/NC 3-5% each.

**SJU airport passenger origin** (Apr 2025): 87% North America, 9% Caribbean, 4.5% Europe + Central + South America combined.

**Lodging split** (FY2023-24): Friends/family 47% (mostly diaspora), **hotels/resorts 22%**, short-term rentals 20%, own home/guesthouse ~11%.

**Takeaway for our site:**
- **EN is unambiguously dominant** for search and bookings. Target = US mainland (English) + US Hispanics (Spanish in US).
- International = growth bet (Spain, LatAm, Canada), not a current revenue driver. But European arrivals from Spain are the fastest-growing segment.

## 2. Cruise passengers vs hotel guests — excursion booking behavior

**Cruise numbers (San Juan Cruise Port):**
- **CY2024:** ~1.43M cruise passengers (transit + homeport)
- **CY2025:** ~1.59M passengers (12% YoY, highest since 2019)
- **FY2023-24 official excursionists:** 1,178,417 cruise passengers in transit; **87% disembark** → ~1.025M actual shore excursionists.
- **Avg cruise excursionist spend:** $69.74 per person.
- **65% of cruise excursions last 5-10 hours** (standard port day).
- Cruise excursionist demographics: 60% women, **age 55-59 (17%) and 65-69 (15%) — older, repeat-cruiser skew**.
- Cruise excursionist education: 55% do not have a high school degree (very different from air visitors — these are mass-market Caribbean cruise passengers, not luxury FIT).

**Hotel/STR visitors** (Discover Puerto Rico 2024 visitor study, n=2,342):
- 66% vacationers, 15% VFR, 7% business.
- 45% in hotels, 34% STRs, ~20% private residences.
- Average stay: 6.1 nights; $208/day spend.
- 64% white/Caucasian, 24% Hispanic/Latino, 13% Black/AA — broader ethnic mix.
- **56% of cruise travelers extend to leisure in PR before/after sailing** — pre/post-cruise hotel guests are a real segment.

**Excursion booking split (cruise):**
The industry guidance is unambiguous: **independent / Viator booking is the dominant path for non-ship excursions.**
- Cruise line markup: 30-100% over independent price.
- Typical independent savings:
  - El Yunque: ship $120-160 vs independent $75-100
  - Bacardí: ship $80-100 vs independent $40-60
  - Old San Juan walking: ship $60-90 vs independent $25-45
- Default rule (cruise industry): "Walk-off port = go independent. Complicated/distant/late return = book through cruise line."
- San Juan specifically = 0-risk walk-off port (piers are inside Old San Juan itself).

**Excursion booking split (hotel):**
Hotel/STR guests book almost entirely independently via Viator/GetYourGuide/direct. Cruise-line desk doesn't reach them at all. They also stay longer (6+ nights) and have higher per-trip spend (~$1,009 per Discover PR).

**Practical split of our potential audience:**

| Segment | Size proxy | Likely booking channel | Language |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cruise passengers (transit, day-trippers) | ~1.0M/yr, mostly 55+ | Independent (Viator); walk Old San Juan free | EN (US) |
| Cruise homeport (overnight, pre/post stay) | ~180-220K/yr, mix | Viator + hotel concierge | EN (US) |
| Hotel guests (US mainland) | ~1.9M hotel-nights/yr (subset of 7.5M total) | Independent, Viator | EN (US) |
| STR guests (US mainland) | High disposable income, $80k+ | Independent, Viator | EN (US) |
| Diaspora (PR in US) | 63% of visitors | Mostly VFR, low excursion use | Mixed EN/ES |
| International — Spain | Fastest-growing (+116% YoY 2025) | Independent (no cruise tie-in) | **ES (Spain-Spanish)** |
| International — Latin America | +47% YoY 2025 | Independent | **ES (LatAm-Spanish)** |
| US Hispanics | 24% of all PR visitors, skews toward VFR + leisure | Independent | ES (US Hispanic) |

## 3. ES pages — who are we actually targeting?

Three distinct ES audiences with **different vocabulary, currency expectation, and intent**:

| Audience | Search volume | Pain points | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Spain-based tourists** | Rapidly growing (Skyscanner: Spain = #2 search origin for PR after US; +116% arrivals YoY 2025; +121% search growth 2026) | Long-haul flight, English barrier on tour pages | **Strong target for ES** — they're already flight-booking, just need content in their language. Note: they're bookers, not VFR. |
| **B. Latin American tourists** (Colombia, DR, Mexico, Chile, Panama, Costa Rica — all in top-10 international list) | Growing (+47% YoY 2025). Search interest in PR surging. | Far flight, US-visa-style friction | **Secondary target.** Smaller absolute numbers but very high intent and growing. |
| **C. US Hispanics / diaspora** | Largest single ES-speaking pool (24% of PR visitors are Hispanic/Latino, 63% identify as Puerto Rican-descent) | Already speak Spanish, but they search in **English** for travel (they're US-based, Google US-edition) | **De-prioritize for pure ES SEO.** Spanish-language content for them is a courtesy/translation layer, not a discovery channel. They book in USD, search in English. |

**Strategic recommendation:**
- **ES pages should primarily target (A) Spain + (B) Latin America — actual Spanish-speaking searchers.**
- Secondary use: translation/UX for US Hispanics who land on a page but already searched in English.
- Don't waste resources on "Mexico-Spanish vs Spain-Spanish" debates for an EN-default market like PR; both dialects work and the international ES tourist pool is still small enough that one neutral ES serves both.

## 4. Currency: USD on the ground — do we need EUR?

**Yes, display prices in EUR for Spain-targeted ES pages** (and probably MXN/ARS/COP for LatAm if we want to go granular, but EUR is the easy win).

- Puerto Rico uses USD. Ground transactions, Viator, everything = USD.
- A Spain-based tourist searching in EUR will see $129 and mentally convert. Showing €119 (≈$129 at ~0.92 EUR/USD) reduces sticker shock.
- Spain has been Discover Puerto Rico's top European growth target (+116% arrivals 2025, +121% searches 2026) and PR is the **official Fitur 2027 guest destination** — they're doubling down on this market. The market expects EUR.
- **Implementation:** Detect by `lang=es` + `?market=es` (or geo-IP), show EUR with a "(≈$129 USD)" note. Don't switch default currency globally — that breaks US-Hispanic/EN pages.

**For Latin America:** USD is actually preferred (or at least common) in Colombia/Mexico/Chile for travel purchases because they compare against international prices. EUR not needed there.

## 5. What language do people actually search Puerto Rico tours in?

**English dominates search volume. By a lot.**

Evidence:
- **Treendly trend data:** ~1,000,000 monthly searches for "Puerto Rico" globally, almost entirely from US/English-speaking markets.
- **Discover Puerto Rico's own Spanish-language site** (descubrepuertorico.com) exists but isn't the primary SEO play.
- **Viator ES market for San Juan** is well-developed (sanjuan.viajarelm.es, musement.com/es/san-juan, etc.) — this is supply responding to demand — but search volume from ES speakers is dwarfed by EN searchers.
- **Search interest correlations:** Top US feeder states (FL, NY, TX) all have ~1,500-3,200 PR searches per 1M residents — orders of magnitude above any individual ES-speaking country.
- **Discover Puerto Rico's IPW 2026 messaging:** The "+116% Spain growth" story is real and large in *percentage* terms, but it comes off a small base. Spain is the #2 searcher globally (Skyscanner), but #2 is a fraction of #1 (US).

**The bottom line:**
- EN pages = **90%+ of addressable search demand**.
- ES pages = **growth bet** for Spain + emerging LatAm market, plus a translation/UX courtesy for US Hispanics.
- The Spain market is the **only one where ES content can plausibly win a meaningful slice of organic search** — they search in Spanish because they live in Spanish.
- US Hispanics mostly search in English even when Spanish-fluent (Google US, English UX, US travel sites).
